# Columbia University: One Year After the $400M Federal Funding Freeze

**Date:** March 2026 | **Prepared for:** Leadership Review

---

## Summary

One year ago, the Trump administration froze $400 million in federal research funding for Columbia University, citing failures to address antisemitism and campus harassment. Columbia chose to negotiate, reaching a preliminary agreement in March 2025 and a final deal in July 2025. The agreements reshaped campus discipline, protest rules, and departmental oversight. Leadership has also changed: acting president Claire Shipman replaced interim president Katrina Armstrong, and incoming president Jennifer Mnookin (formerly of UW-Madison) begins in July 2026. The campus climate has shifted noticeably, though some commitments remain unfulfilled and legal challenges are pending.

---

## Key Points

**Student Discipline**
- Disciplinary oversight moved from the faculty-led university senate to the provost's office; students no longer sit on judicial panels.
- Over 70 students who occupied Butler Library in May 2025 were expelled, suspended, or had degrees revoked — punishments widely criticized as disproportionate.
- A state judge vacated punishments for the 2024 Hamilton Hall occupation, ruling Columbia violated state law and its own rules. An appeal is under consideration.

**Protest Rules**
- Masks are not banned, but masked protesters who refuse to identify themselves face arrest.
- Protests in academic buildings are now prohibited; 10 days' notice is required for events exceeding 25 attendees.
- Rules are stricter but remain ambiguous — faculty critics note broad language could inadvertently restrict lawful outdoor protest.
- Chilling effects are reported: student organizers increasingly hold events off-campus.

**Middle East Studies**
- Columbia resisted placing the Middle East, South Asian and African Studies (MESAAS) department under receivership, citing academic freedom.
- A review committee recommended hiring more social-science faculty with Middle East expertise — framed as filling gaps across departments, not targeting MESAAS.
- MESAAS faculty critical of Israel continue to teach; academic independence has not been formally curtailed.

**Other Commitments**
- Columbia adopted the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance (IHRA) definition of antisemitism for disciplinary use. Critics argue it chills academic discourse; at least one professor canceled a planned course as a result.
- Campus organizations were required to formally renounce ties with the protest coalition CUAD to retain university recognition.
- Two non-binding March 2025 pledges were not carried forward: a Tel Aviv Global Hub program (delayed indefinitely) and a K-12 antisemitism curriculum (de-prioritized).

---

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Clarify protest rules.** Ambiguous language creates legal exposure and inconsistent enforcement. Convene a faculty-administration working group to produce clearer, legally sound guidelines before the next academic year.

2. **Resolve pending litigation.** The Hamilton Hall appeal decision will set precedent for all subsequent disciplinary actions. Engage legal counsel to assess risk and establish a consistent framework before fall 2026.

3. **Review IHRA implementation.** Commission an independent assessment of whether IHRA adoption has materially affected academic discourse, and establish clear guidelines distinguishing protected speech from policy violations.

4. **Restore faculty governance balance.** Consider a limited advisory role for the senate in protest rule-making to rebuild trust without ceding administrative authority.

5. **Set timelines for deferred commitments.** Establish and publish a revised timeline for the Tel Aviv Global Hub and evaluate whether the K-12 curriculum initiative can be re-scoped and resourced.

6. **Leverage incoming leadership.** Use Jennifer Mnookin's July start as an opportunity to reset relationships with faculty, students, and the federal government with a clear, unified institutional message.
